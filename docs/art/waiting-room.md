# Waiting room — specification

Source of truth for the lobby backdrop: what is actually in the reference image, what is
actually in `assets/sprites/room.svg` and `assets/sprites/temple.svg`, and how those plus
authored vector geometry produce image A's read without disagreeing with the wall bitboard
by a single tile.

Nothing here is implemented. Every number says how it was obtained. Where a measurement
failed, it says that instead of guessing (§7.3).

**Chain impact: none.** This layer reads `MAP_GRID`, `GATE_*` and `LOBBY_SPAWN_*` out of
`@heartrot/client` and writes nothing. No instruction, no account, no compute unit. The ER
speed the user asked to protect is untouched by construction — see §9.

Companion specs: `docs/art/temple-scene.md` (the arena backdrop, image B),
`docs/art/knights.md` (the avatars). Where they fixed a number, this document uses theirs
and says so.

## 1. What is actually in image A

`/home/anshtyagi/Downloads/waiting_area_full_vertical.png`, 1122×785, opened and inspected
at full size and in crops.

**Construction, outside in.**

- **The void.** Everything outside the room is near-black with a violet bias: sampled
  (10.0, 9.1, 21.6), L 10.2, over an 180×70 box at the top-left. It is not neutral black,
  and it is not blue.
- **The wall.** Two courses. An outer course of large light-grey bricks, sampled
  (40.1, 31.7, 46.8) L 34.6 along the top; and an inner course of dark carved stone,
  (21.0, 16.0, 31.3) L 18.1, which is where the alcoves, chains and banner mounts sit. The
  side walls read the same but darker, (32.7, 26.4, 38.3) L 28.6, because no torch faces
  them squarely. The wall is roughly 1/9 of the image height — thick, not a line.
- **The gate,** top centre. Bottom to top: a heavy iron portcullis set in a stone arch; a
  horizontal red **BOSS FIGHT** sign, sampled (85.8, 27.2, 34.7) — the most saturated red
  in the frame and the only text; a ram/goat skull crest above the sign; a lit brazier on
  each side of the sign. The whole assembly is about 22% of the image width and it is the
  only thing in the room with its own light.
- **Banners.** Two purple hangings on the top wall, flanking the gate, bearing a horned
  skull. Sampled (29.0, 19.8, 38.4) L 23.1 — dark, low-chroma, closer to the wall than to
  the gate's red. They read as texture, not as a second focal point.
- **Torches.** Small orange wall flames around the whole perimeter, each with an elliptical
  warm pool on the floor below it. The pool sampled (50.2, 35.0, 40.8) L 38.7 — note
  R−B = **+9.4**, warm, against a floor that is cold everywhere else.
- **Props.** Wooden barrels and a treasure chest at the top-left, barrels stacked at the
  top-right and lower-right, a candle cluster and scattered bones at the lower-left, all
  tucked against a wall. Nothing stands in the middle of the floor.
- **The floor.** Grey-violet stone blocks with diamond medallions inlaid at intervals,
  sampled (66.7, 52.0, 64.1) L 56.0 at the lit centre, falling to (34.0, 29.7, 48.1) L 31.9
  near the bottom. Interior median L 41.1.
- **The vignette.** Heavy. Whole-image luminance percentiles are p1 1.7, p5 5.9, p25 12.9,
  **p50 28.5**, p75 42.3, p95 59.6. Half the picture is below L 28.5.
- **The stairs,** bottom centre, entering the room.

**The one fact that decides the palette.** The brief's phrase is "warm torchlight against
cold stone", and the sampling says the *stone* is not warm at all: over the interior the
floor's mean **R−B = −8.3** and **G−B = −16.8**. Blue is the highest channel, green the
lowest — a desaturated violet-mauve. Every warm value in the image belongs to a *light
source* (torch pool R−B = +9.4, the sign, the flames). Grading the stone warm is the
mistake this section exists to prevent.

Method for every number above: `PIL` → `numpy`, mean over a named pixel box, and
`0.2126R + 0.7152G + 0.0722B` for L. Boxes are listed with their coordinates so any of it
can be re-read.

## 2. The frame

### 2.1 What must be inside it

From `packages/client/src/map.ts` (generated by `tools/gen_map.py`; never restate these):

| thing | world units |
|---|---|
| gate block | x 480..543, y 608..639 |
| every lobby spawn | x 208..664 at y 832 |
| walkable lobby floor | x 16..1007, y 640..1007 |

Measured off `assets/map/arena.json` this session:

- The lobby floor is **1,322 walkable tiles**, tile x 1..62 by tile y 40..62.
- Tile rows 36 and 37 are wall at every x **except** tiles 30..33 — the pit doorway,
  directly above the gate.
- Tile row 63 is **solid wall across its whole width**. There is no bottom doorway.

### 2.2 The top edge is derived, not chosen

Image A wants vertical room above the gate for the sign, the crest and the braziers. How
far up can the layer paint stone without lying?

- Rows 36–37 (world y 576..607) are wall everywhere but the doorway. Painting stone there
  **agrees** with the bitboard.
- Rows 34–35 (world y 544..575) are `P` — walkable pit floor — across most of their width.
  Painting stone there is a wall that is not there.

So **world y 576 is the hard top edge**, and it gives exactly 32 units (2 tiles) of wall
above the gate band for the whole gate assembly's furniture. That is a constraint, not a
preference; do not raise it.

### 2.3 The viewBox

```
viewBox="-48 560 1120 496"   preserveAspectRatio="xMidYMid meet"
```

- **x −48..1072** shows 48 units of masonry outside the playfield on each side. Off-map is
  solid by `isWallTile`'s own contract ("Off-map reads as wall"), no player can stand there,
  and it is what turns the map's 16-unit border into a wall with thickness. Image A's wall
  is ~1/9 of the frame height; a bare 16-unit border is ~1/28 and reads as a line.
- **y 560..1056** puts 16 units of ceiling shadow above the wall top and 32 units of
  masonry below the bottom border.
- Aspect **2.26:1**.

The conservative alternative is `0 576 1024 448` (aspect 2.29) which shows only map space.
It was rendered first (§7.1) and the side walls read as thin dark strips. Prefer the
extended frame.

`meet`, never `slice`. The complaint being answered is a crop; `slice` reintroduces it.

### 2.4 `usePixelFit` forces a square and must change

`app/src/render/Arena.tsx:927`:

```js
const raw = (Math.min(r.width, r.height) * dpr) / ARENA_UNITS;
const css = `${(ARENA_UNITS * scale) / dpr}px`;
s.style.width = css;
s.style.height = css;
```

Both axes get the same `css`, sized off `Math.min(width, height)`. The SVG element is
therefore always a **square sized to the viewport's short edge**. With a 1120×496 viewBox
and `meet` inside a square element, the scale is `S/1120` and the room renders `S` wide by
`S × 496/1120` tall — on a 1920×1080 screen that is 1080 wide inside a 1920 viewport, 44%
of the width unused. This hook is the second half of "the map is not full screen"; the
camera transform is the first.

The fix is to fit the frame's own aspect:

```
scale = min(r.width / FRAME_W, r.height / FRAME_H)      // FRAME_W = 1120, FRAME_H = 496
width  = FRAME_W * scale ; height = FRAME_H * scale
```

`crisp` keeps its current meaning (`raw >= 1`), computed from the same `scale`. The frame
constants must come from one exported pair, because the lobby and the fight have different
ones and this is the exact shape of the "one fact stored twice" defect the project keeps
paying for.

### 3.1 `temple.svg` — the floor, free

The lower band (source rows 134..238, which is world y 576..1024 under `Scene.tsx`'s

with a broad staircase at bottom centre** — image A's floor, already drawn.

Measured: the stair's two bright side rails occupy source columns **75..84 and 126..135**,
i.e. world **x 366..663**, centred at **x 514**. The gate's centre is x 511.5. The temple's
own stair is centred within **3 world units** of the gate, so the aisle from the stair to
the doorway is already dead centre. This is luck, and it should be used rather than

Reuse `TEMPLE_PATHS` and `Scene.tsx`'s existing transform verbatim. **Zero new bytes** —
the module is already in the graph. Only the CSS `filter` differs (§4).

Image A's stairs enter through the bottom wall. **They cannot.** Tile row 63 is solid wall
across its full width (§2.1), and a painted opening in a wall the chain raycasts is the
signature defect. The temple's own stair stands in as a floor feature at the foot of the
aisle instead. Do not cut a hole in row 63; if a real bottom entrance is ever wanted, it is
an edit to `assets/map/arena.json` and a `gen_map.py` re-run, not an art change.

### 3.2 `room.svg` — usable only as opaque panels

`viewBox 0 0 279 168`, **52 paths**, **6,763 rects**, 100% coverage. Imported by nothing
today. Raw 103,214 B, brotli 19,831 B.

It parses with **`tools/svg_slice.py`'s `parse()` unmodified** — verified this session,
returns `279 × 168, 6763 rects`. That matters because `svg_slice._RECT` matches absolute
`M` only; `temple.svg` uses the relative `m` form and raises
`path d= holds commands this tool cannot read`. So `room.svg` is the one checked-in asset
the existing slicer can already read, and a props generator would be `parse()` plus a rect
list, in the shape `tools/gen_knights.py` already established.

Rasterised at 4× and read with a coordinate grid, it is a top-down torchlit dungeon room
containing, at these source rects:

| thing | source rect (x, y, w, h) |
|---|---|
| wall torch, bracket + flame | (21, 43, 8, 15) and (250, 49, 8, 12) |
| open chest, gold | (40, 39, 19, 21) |
| crate / barrel | (59, 41, 19, 19) |
| bone on the floor | (45, 60, 14, 10) |
| wall alcove, framed niche | (42, 20, 16, 18) |
| **skull crest** | (107, 2, 18, 15) |
| wooden door with gold lintel | (104, 16, 24, 24) |
| light brick wall course | y 15..21, full width |
| dark carved inner course | y 21..38, full width |
| boulder / rubble course | y 140..150 |

It also contains two knights, a fire elemental, two rats, a key and a wall skeleton. **None
of those may ship.**

Native scale: its bricks and floor stones read at roughly 8 source px per tile, so
**1 room.svg pixel = 2 world units**.

**The negative result that decides the strategy.** The props cannot be cut out of their
background. Keying by colour fails because the palette is shared — `#a7939c` is both the
wall brick and the chest's metal trim. Keying by connectivity fails too: a flood fill from
the crop border over the thirteen large-area fills
(`#5b5076 #876f7a #756278 #220f29 #3c3055 #251845 #090018 #53404e #a7939c #d4bcd1 #ece0eb
#cbc3c6 #ac8193`) leaves

```
alcove   0 / 360 px kept
bone     2 / 192
crest    4 / 340
torch   18 / 170
chest   69 / 483
crate   69 / 441
door   307 / 676
```

because the props are *drawn in the same stone and bone greys as the wall and floor behind
them*. The flood reaches into every prop and erases it. Round 1 of the prototype (§7.1)
shows the other failure: sliced opaque, every prop lands on the floor inside a visible grey
rectangle.

**Therefore: `room.svg` ships as opaque, tile-aligned panels placed only over wall tiles,
never as a cut-out sprite standing on floor.** On a wall tile the panel's background *is*
the wall, so there is nothing to key. Use it for:

- the top wall band's brick texture (y 608..639, tiled from source (30, 15, 60, 25) and
  (150, 15, 60, 25) alternating),
- the wall alcoves, from source (40, 15, 20, 25),
- the portcullis, from source (98, 10, 34, 30), over the gate at world x 464..560, y 600..640.

Cost, measured: those five panels emit 26,636 B raw, **4,849 B brotli**. That is 6.3% of the
art's current 77.4 KiB brotli and 0.7% of the 695.3 KiB critical path.

### 3.3 Everything else is authored vector

Floor-standing props (barrels, chest, bones, candles) and the torch flames are authored
geometry, not slices, for three reasons: the key-out above is impossible; a 19×21 source
sprite blown up 2.2× is a blurry blob at this frame size; and they must scale with the
frame while the wall panels are pinned to tiles.

Measured: the five prop generators (barrel, chest, bones, candles, torch) are **33 lines,
2,301 B raw, 623 B brotli** — 7.8× cheaper than the five wall panels, and with no generator,
no new asset, no `.gen.ts` and no `--check` mode to maintain.

Each is primitives only: a barrel is a shadow ellipse, a rounded rect, two hoop rects and
two ellipses for the lid; a torch is a shaft rect and three flame paths. See §7.2 for the
exact shapes that were rendered and checked.

`ponytail:` authored vector props, no `tools/gen_props.py`. Ceiling: they do not carry
`room.svg`'s dithering, so at a very close zoom they will read smoother than the wall
panels beside them. Upgrade path is hand-authored per-prop alpha rect lists in a JSON that
`svg_slice.parse` consumes — the pattern `svg_slice.PARTS` already uses — not a colour key
and not a flood fill, both of which are measured above to fail.

## 4. The grade — fitted, not chosen

`Scene.tsx` grades the same 16 temple paths with
`sepia(1) hue-rotate(177deg) saturate(1.39) brightness(0.5)`. Applied to the **lobby band**
(source rows 134..238), area-weighted by that band's own pixel counts, it lands at

```
sRGB (31, 38, 48)   L 37.0   R-B = -17.8
```

Green above red, blue highest: a cold blue-green cavern. That is exactly the read the user
called wrong, and it is the shipped lobby today.

The warm grade was **fitted, not picked**, by the same method `Scene.tsx` documents:

1. Take image A's own luminance ramp. Crop the room interior (`[140:700, 60:1060]`), drop
   every pixel with `max(RGB) − min(RGB) ≥ 45` so flames, the red sign and gold do not
   pollute the stone ramp — 556,252 px survive, L 3.9..68.1 — and bin them into 16
   luminance bins, taking the mean RGB of each.
2. Sort `temple.svg`'s 16 palette entries by luminance (L 18.2..113.8) and map each entry's
   normalised luminance `u` to that ramp at `u^1.15`, the same gamma `temple-scene.md` §5
   uses.
3. Weight each entry by its **pixel share of the lobby band only** — measured
   `#161620` 30.7%, `#565854` 21.0%, `#5c605c` 12.9%, `#494a49` 8.4%, `#34363d` 7.3% — not
   by its share of the whole asset, because the fight and the lobby see different halves.
4. Least-squares the four CSS Filter Effects matrices (`sepia`/`hue-rotate`/`saturate`/
   `brightness`, evaluated in sRGB as the shorthand functions specify) against that target:
   coarse grid, then 4,000 annealed local steps.

Result:

```css
filter: sepia(0.81) hue-rotate(232deg) saturate(1.58) brightness(0.45);
```

```
weighted RMSE          3.94 / 255 per channel   (Scene.tsx's cold fit reports 3.44)
lobby band, graded     sRGB (37, 30, 42)   L 32.1   R-B = -4.8
image A floor                              L 41.1   R-B = -8.3
```

Red above green, blue highest — the reference's own violet-mauve, and the opposite of the
shipped grade's channel order. It sits slightly less blue than the reference (−4.8 against
−8.3), which is correct: the torch pools of §5 push it warmer still, and fitting the *base*
to the reference's *already-lit* mean would double-count the light.

The same filter goes on the `room.svg` wall panels. Without it they sit ungraded next to
graded temple stone and read as a lighter, bluer strip — visible in §7.2's render, where the
top wall band measures L 0.1172 against a floor of 0.0328.

## 5. The lighting stack

Painted **over** the graded stone and **not through** the grade filter: the added light is
already the colour it should be, and running it through `sepia`+`hue-rotate` would grade the
torchlight along with the wall. Same rule `Scene.tsx` states for its own stack.

Back to front:

1. **Void** — `#0a0710` over the whole frame. Image A's outside sampled (10, 9, 22); this is
   that, rounded.
2. **Outer masonry** — `#241d2c` in the three off-map bands the extended frame exposes
   (x −48..0, x 1024..1072, y 1024..1056).
3. **Graded temple** (§4).
4. **Ambient floor lift** — one radial, warm, centred on the room. This is the room's own
   bounce, not a lamp.
5. **Walls**, from the bitboard (§6).
6. **Torch pools** — one `<radialGradient>` in the default `objectBoundingBox` units, and
   one `<ellipse>` per torch filling with `url(#pool)`. The gradient scales to each
   ellipse's own box, so **ten lights cost one gradient def**. Warm: `#ffc477` at the core
   through `#ff8f33` to transparent.
   Painted **after** the walls, so the light lands on the masonry as well as the floor. In
   §7.1 they were painted before and the wall ate them.
7. **The gate glow** — a red radial on the gate assembly, `#ff2b3c`. The only red in the
   frame, and the reason the eye goes there.
8. **Vignette** — `cx 50% cy 50% r 72%`, transparent to 0.50, then `#07040c` at 0.80.

Ten torches: four on the top wall at world x 196/324/700/828, y 636; two on each side wall
at y 712 and y 888; two braziers flanking the gate at x 452 and x 572, y 628. Every one sits
on a wall tile. The top-wall four are clear of x 416..607 so they never overlap the gate
assembly.

## 6. The walls, and the rule that governs every prop

The walls are **compiled from `MAP_GRID`**, never drawn. `app/src/render/sprites.ts` already
exports `MAP_WALL_PATH` and `MAP_RIM_PATH` from its `tilePath` helper; reuse them, and add
two more from the same helper rather than a second geometry source:

- `MAP_FACE_PATH` — a 3-unit lit strip on the left and right edge of any wall tile with
  floor beside it. `MAP_RIM_PATH` only lights walls with floor *above*, so today the left and
  right borders get no highlight at all and read as flat strips.
- `MAP_FOOT_PATH` — a 4-unit dark strip on the bottom edge of any wall tile with floor below
  it, which is the shadow the wall casts into the room.

Measured off the generated grid, the lobby's walls are:

- the border, 16 units thick on all four sides;
- **26 interior pillar blocks, every one exactly 2×2 tiles (32×32 world units)**, at
  y 672, 736, 800, 864, 928 — six per row at tile columns 6, 14, 22, 40, 48, 56 on rows
  672/800/928, and four per row at columns 10, 18, 44, 52 on rows 736/864;
- and therefore a **clear central aisle, tile x 24..39 (world x 384..639), 256 units wide,
  unobstructed in every row**, running from the bottom of the room to the gate.

That aisle is the composition. The stair (§3.1) sits in it, the gate closes it, and every
prop and torch stays out of it.

**The prop rule, and it is not negotiable.** Art that disagrees with the bitboard is a
player walking through a painted wall or bumping into open floor. So:

- Anything with height — barrel, chest, crate, portcullis, alcove, banner, torch — is placed
  so that **at least half its footprint covers wall tiles**. In practice that means against
  the border's inner face or on the top wall band, which is where image A puts them anyway.
- Anything flat — bones, candle bases, floor decals — may sit anywhere on floor, because
  walking over a bone reads correctly.
- Nothing at all goes in the central aisle, and nothing goes within 32 units of the gate
  block or of the spawn row `y = 832`.

`ponytail:` props overlap the wall's inner face rather than getting their own collision.
Ceiling: a knight can clip the outer 16 units of a barrel. Upgrade path is marking those
tiles `#` in `assets/map/arena.json` and re-running `gen_map.py` — which changes the chain's
wall table, so it is a map change with a `cargo test` behind it, not an art change.

The 26 pillars must **not** be filled with `PAL.wall` (`#2b2733`). In §7.2's render they are
the only cool objects in a warm frame and read as flat tiles floating on the floor rather
than as columns. Fill them from the graded floor's own family with a warm rim on the
torch-facing edge.

## 7. What was prototyped, and what it proved

Three rounds were generated as standalone HTML and rendered in real Chrome (Playwright from
`/home/anshtyagi/.npm/_npx/9833c18b2d85bc59`, 1600×900, `deviceScaleFactor 1`). Generators
and screenshots are throwaway and live in the scratchpad.

### 7.1 Round 1 — cut-out props, pools under the walls

180 elements. Proved three defects at once: every sliced prop sat inside a visible grey
rectangle of its own background; the torch pools were painted before the walls and were
covered by them; and `PAL.wall` pillars read as floating squares. It also proved the
composition is right — gate at top centre with the sign, banners flanking it, props in the
corners, the aisle running up the middle.

### 7.2 Round 3 — opaque wall panels, authored floor props

269 SVG nodes, 330,806 B of markup. The props now read cleanly at any scale, the top wall
band carries real pixel-art brick, the pools land on the masonry, and the room reads as a
warm-lit violet stone chamber with one red thing in it. Remaining defects, all listed as
fixes above: the `room.svg` panel is ungraded (§4), the pillars are cool (§6), and the pools
are too strong (§7.3).

### 7.3 The pool-opacity sweep failed — reported, not guessed

Legibility was measured off the round-3 render by mapping world coordinates back through
the `meet` transform, converting sRGB to linear and taking WCAG relative luminance, then
comparing against the knight skin luminances `Scene.tsx` already cites (Cobalt 0.1678,
Argent 0.1303, Nocturne 0.1136):

```
floor at the spawn row (y 832)     sRGB (54.7, 45.3, 51.2)   Y 0.0328
floor, centre aisle below spawn         (55.4, 45.2, 53.6)   Y 0.0315
floor, inside a torch pool              (91.0, 62.5, 55.8)   Y 0.0617
floor, darkest corner                   (24.6, 21.3, 23.0)   Y 0.0084
top wall band (room.svg, ungraded)      (107.8, 74.6, 69.0)  Y 0.1172

                spawn row   centre   torch pool   dark corner
  Cobalt          2.63:1     2.67:1    1.95:1       3.73:1
  Argent          2.18:1     2.21:1    1.61:1       3.09:1
  Nocturne        1.97:1     2.01:1    1.46:1       2.80:1
```

**The torch pools brighten the floor into the knights.** Nocturne inside a pool is 1.46:1 —
worse than the 1.85:1 the fight scene achieves after `Scene.tsx` moved `PIT_POOL_ALPHA`
*down* for exactly this reason. Legibility changing as you walk past a torch is the same
defect that file solved once already.

The constraint that follows is arithmetic, not taste. For the darkest skin to hold the
fight scene's 1.85:1:

```
(0.1136 + 0.05) / (Y + 0.05) >= 1.85   =>   Y <= 0.0384
```

**The lit floor must not exceed Y = 0.0384 anywhere, torch pools included.** Round 3's pools
peak at 0.0617 and are ~1.6× too bright.

A sweep at pool opacity 0.40 / 0.16 / 0.11 was run to find the value that lands on 0.0384.
**It produced three identical readings and was a failed measurement, not a result** — the
`sed` that parameterised the generator mangled it, the page rendered wrong, and all three
rows read Y 0.3507.

**SUPERSEDED — the sweep was re-run properly and the number is specified now.** Over 323,063
legal stands rather than four sample boxes:

| POOL_ALPHA | 0.045 | 0.060 | **0.070** | 0.090 | 0.130 | 0.180 | 0.220 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| max floor Y | 0.0340 | 0.0366 | **0.0382** | 0.0425 | 0.0510 | 0.0630 | 0.0746 |
| Nocturne | 1.95:1 | 1.89:1 | **1.86:1** | 1.77:1 | 1.62:1 | 1.45:1 | 1.31:1 |

**Ceiling 0.07; shipped 0.045 at Nocturne 1.95:1.** The authoritative write-up — including
the finding that what actually broke the cap was paint and not a lamp (a closed `rect` arch
outline laying half a 3-unit stroke across the walkable gate block, Nocturne 1.76:1 on the
one tile every player holds still on) — is the header of `app/src/render/WaitingRoom.tsx`,
and `17-fullscreen-spec.md` §2.1 now carries the same table. The boot check at the foot of
that file is the standing guard.

### 7.4 Paint cost — measured

**SUPERSEDED for the shipped layer** by the paired re-measure recorded in
`WaitingRoom.tsx`'s header, and the two are not comparable row to row: this table is a
269-node ROUND-3 PROTOTYPE against a 16.7 ms vsync, the re-measure is the shipped arch on a
6.9 ms-refresh box. As it ships (never invalidated) the shipped layer runs rAF p50 6.9 /
p95 7.0 / max 9.0 ms with 0 frames missed, identical at 1× and 6× CPU throttle and identical
to an *empty* rAF loop. Forced to fully repaint every frame it runs p50 20.9 / p95 27.8 at
both rates, measured paired — both forms of the arch in one page, swapped in the DOM,
interleaved A/B/A/B/B/A — at p50 20.90 ms in all six runs and 259 nodes either way. The
round-3 numbers below are kept because they are the reason the layer is built the way it is:

Round 3, 269 nodes, **forced to fully repaint every frame** by writing a transform on the
layer's own node, 240 frames, against a 16.7 ms vsync:

| | rAF p50 | rAF p95 | rAF max | load | TaskDuration |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| cpu 1× | 16.7 ms | 16.9 ms | 24.5 ms | 49 ms | 0.171 s |
| cpu 6× | 16.6 ms | 17.4 ms | 17.8 ms | 41 ms | 0.633 s |

**Zero frames missed at either CPU rate**, including under the 6× throttle the frame-budget
review uses as its slow-machine proxy. p95 sits 0.7 ms over vsync at 6×, which is jitter,
not a dropped frame.

That is a worst case that never occurs, because the shipped layer never repaints (§8). For
scale, `docs/review/frame-budget.md` reports the whole shipped scene at 20 knights, full
art, 6× throttle at p50 9.38 / p95 14.92 ms, and the existing scene-only harness rows
(`scripts/spike/framebudget/results_scene_before.json`) report the arena's 44-node temple
layer at `commitP50` 0 and `buildMs` 2.4. This layer is 269 nodes against that 44, and still
free.

## 8. Mounting, and how it stays off the per-notification path

Identical contract to `Scene.tsx`, and for the identical reason. Four barriers:

1. **A module-scope `ReactElement`, not a component.** `export const WAITING: ReactElement =
   (...)`. React compares it by identity on every update, finds it `===` the previous one,
   and skips the subtree without diffing a node. No props object, no hook, no dependency
   array to get wrong. `Scene.tsx` measures the alternative at 11.2 ms/frame and 3/3
   renderer crashes at 300 frames.
2. **Nothing in it derives from chain state.** Every number comes from a build constant, the
   generated `MAP_GRID`, `GATE_*`/`LOBBY_SPAWN_*`, or a measurement in this document. The
   68.4% of `Players` notifications that carry no position change, and the Magic Router's
   duplicate delivery of every one of them, have nothing to write here.
3. **`will-change: transform`,** so it holds its own compositor raster. Promoted vs
   unpromoted is measured at 30–85× and it is the whole game.
4. **`pointer-events: none`,** so it can never become a hit-test target and drag a style
   recalculation in through the input path.

It mounts as the first child of `#camera`, in the place `{SCENE}` occupies. The two are
mutually exclusive — `{lobby ? WAITING : SCENE}` off one boolean derived from the local
seat's `zone`, which flips once per raid. Rendering both and letting the viewBox hide one
wastes raster on an offscreen layer.

**The camera.** `CAM_ARENA`, `LOBBY_ZOOM`, `LOBBY_SPAN`, `LOBBY_X`, `LOBBY_Y`,
`LOBBY_MARGIN` and the follow logic in the rAF loop are all deleted by this layer's
existence: the frame fits the room, so there is nothing to pan and nothing to crop. That
also deletes the defect class those two bug reports came from — a player outside the window
is invisible. The `<svg>`'s `viewBox` becomes the one thing that changes with zone, and it
is two static strings, set as an attribute, never animated. `Arena.tsx`'s own note that
animating `viewBox` measured worse still holds and is not contradicted: this swaps it once.

The gate-passage animation the user asked for is a separate deliverable and owns the
crossfade between the two frames. This document only guarantees that both frames exist and
that neither crops.

## 9. Chain impact

None, and this is the section the "do not degrade the ER speed" constraint is answered in.

- No instruction is added, changed or called. No account is read or written. The layer's
  compute-unit cost is **zero**, so `boss_tick`'s measured 24,884 CU of a 399,700 ceiling at
  20 players is untouched, and so are `shoot`'s 781 CU of guards.
- No new subscription, no new poll, no new send. The write-to-visible p50 of 122 ms at 20
  seats is a property of the send path and the blockhash cache in
  `packages/client/src/connection.ts`; nothing here touches either.
- `MOVE_MS = 50` and `state::TICK_MS` are not read by this layer and not changed by it.
- The layer contains no generated file and writes none. `MAP_GRID`, `GATE_*` and
  `LOBBY_SPAWN_*` are consumed read-only from `@heartrot/client`. Nothing here is a second
  copy of a fact `gen_map.py` owns.

The one thing that *could* have touched chain state is the prop rule of §6 — marking prop
tiles solid would change the wall bitboard. It is explicitly **not** specified, and the
upgrade path names the `gen_map.py` re-run it would require.

## 10. Acceptance

A build satisfies this document when:

1. The lobby frame is `-48 560 1120 496` with `preserveAspectRatio="xMidYMid meet"`, and
   `usePixelFit` sizes the element to that aspect rather than to a square (§2.4).
2. No camera transform is applied in the lobby, and nothing pans.
3. Every one of the 1,322 walkable lobby floor tiles, the gate block, and all 20 spawn
   positions on `y = 832` are inside the frame, and no painted stone covers a walkable tile
   (verify by compositing `MAP_WALL_PATH` over the render and diffing).
4. The graded lobby band, area-weighted, has **R > G** — the reference's channel order — not
   the shipped `G > R`.
5. The brightest floor sample anywhere in the room, torch pools included, is
   **Y ≤ 0.0384** (§7.3).
6. The layer is a module-scope element, carries `will-change: transform` and
   `pointer-events: none`, and re-renders zero times under a live notification feed.
7. Nothing from `room.svg` ships except opaque panels sitting on wall tiles — in particular
   none of its two knights, fire elemental, two rats, key or wall skeleton.
