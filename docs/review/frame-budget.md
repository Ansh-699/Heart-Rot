# frame-budget: the shipped HEARTROT scene measured in a real browser at 1/5/10/20 knights

## Findings

Cap VISIBLE bullets 128 → 32 in `app/src/render/Arena.tsx:463` (`arena.bullets.map(...)` — slice or skip past 32 active slots when drawing). Do not touch `MAX_BULLETS = 128` or `bullets_per_volley = 3 + alive_count`; those are chain facts and a render cap must not change what the client believes is on the board. Measured at 20 knights, full art, the real 714 notifications/s feed, 6× CPU throttle: p50 11.46 → 9.38 ms, p95 16.96 → 14.92 ms, frames over 16.7 ms 7.2% → 4.6%, notifications actually serviced 61/s → 72/s. Nothing else on the list is worth half of it, and spec §9.3's own documented trigger for this lever ("if p95 ever crosses ~12 ms") has already fired — p95 is 14.02 ms at the crank rate and 16.96 ms at the real feed rate.

Second, ship the `subscribe.ts` payload dedupe of spec §9.4 — it is NOT implemented (`app/src/net/subscribe.ts` `handle()` decodes and dispatches every message unconditionally; there is no payload-string comparison anywhere in the file). It is worth 3.12 → 2.50 ms p50 on the unthrottled box, a ~20% main-thread saving, and it cannot cost anything. But say plainly when it lands that it does NOT rescue a slow machine: at 6× throttle 372 Hz and 714 Hz both saturate at ~62 notifications/s serviced and read the same frame time within σ.

Third, three explicit do-nots, each measured: do not reduce the boss to `--scale 2` (S=3 costs 2.00 ms p50 / 3.10 ms p95 alone, affordable — spec §13 risk 1 is closed); do not rasterise or simplify the temple (0.08 ms p50, free, on a second independent harness); do not hoist the 128 inline bullet `ref` closures at `Arena.tsx:467` (A/B'd against a byte-identical copy with only that change, 5 reps at two feed rates and two CPU rates: worth nothing in either direction — the bullet cost is raster, not reconciliation).

## On-chain

Nothing in this task is on chain, and that is the point of the recommendation's shape. The visible-bullet cap is PURELY COSMETIC and must stay that way: `MAX_BULLETS = 128` and `bullets_per_volley = 3 + alive_count` are chain facts that decide damage, and the spec's own line is "cap DRAWING, never simulation, or the client disagrees with the crank about what is on the board". The change is one expression in `Arena.tsx`'s render — how many of the 128 pooled slots get a `<line>` — and the frame loop's extrapolation and the swept-collision damage the crank computes are untouched. A player on a capped client and a player on an uncapped one are still playing the same game on the same tick; they just see a different number of tracers.

The `subscribe.ts` dedupe is likewise entirely client-local: it drops a byte-identical base64 payload before the decoder runs. It removes no information because the duplicate carries none — every trigger in the animation contract (§7.4) is already a value diff against the last consumed payload, so a dropped duplicate diffs to nothing that a kept one would not have. Its three constraints from §9.4 still bind: compare per account kind before decoding, reset the cache on every socket `open` (the snapshot-on-open must never be suppressed by a payload cached from before a disconnect), and let a duplicate still count as liveness.

The two "do not" findings also stay off chain by construction. `BOSS_SCALE = 3` lives in `packages/client/src/hitboxes.ts` and is emitted by `tools/gen_hitboxes.py` alongside `PART_HITBOXES`, `CORE_*` and `MUZZLES` — so it IS on chain in the sense that changing it moves every hitbox and every muzzle the crank spawns from. That is exactly why the measurement mattered: had S=3 failed the frame budget, the fix would have been a generator re-run touching Rust and TS together, not a render tweak. It passed at 2.00 ms p50, so nothing moves.

One thing this measurement makes an on-chain-adjacent argument about: the client cannot service the notification rate the chain produces at 20 seats on a mid-range machine (61 of 714/s). The fix is not a chain change — spec §12.12 already measured that splitting `Players` or changing the Arena account role buys zero latency and a worse tail, and option A (dropping Arena from `move`'s account list, −38.6% traffic) is explicitly a guard decision rather than a performance one. The fix is client-side: fewer drawn nodes per render and the dedupe.

## Risks

- BLOCKER, unrelated to this task but found by it: `cargo test -p heartrot` does not compile. `error[E0425]: cannot find function \`begin_muster\` in module \`handlers::settle\``. The 59-test baseline in the brief cannot currently be read, and I could not verify it. I touched no Rust; this is another slice's in-flight edit (spec §6.1 renames tag 3 to `begin_muster`). Whoever owns that slice must land the handler.
- `tools/gen_knights.py` does not exist and no `<defs>` in the tree carries a `k*-rest` id, so `Knight.tsx`'s `<use href="#k0-rest">` resolves to nothing and twenty knights currently draw twenty shadow ellipses. Every knight-art number here is a delta from a COST MODEL of that generator's output (`scripts/spike/framebudget/knightdefs.py`, half-res 33x42, 5 poses x 3 skins, 4,302 subpaths / 65,883 bytes — against spec §8.2's independent ~65 KB estimate), not from its output. Re-run the harness once the generator lands; 4,302 subpaths is the number it must not exceed.
- Every number is from one Fedora/X11 box, Chrome 151, dPR 1.0, 144 Hz. No Firefox, no WebKit, no mobile GPU, no dPR 2. A dPR-2 display quadruples rasterised area and the p95 headroom available to absorb it is 0.98x at the real feed rate — this is the most likely way the result gets worse, and it is the same gap render-scale.md flagged and nobody has closed.
- `cpu6` slows script and main-thread raster but does NOT slow the GPU. A device that is GPU-bound rather than CPU-bound is not modelled by any row here, and the S=3 boss covering most of the frame is exactly the kind of load that would find one.
- Bullets were active in all 128 slots for the whole run. The pool drops a spawn when full and the live instrument saw only 13-15 in flight at 20 seats (spec §12.11), so the 2.94 ms bullet cost is an UPPER BOUND. This cuts both ways: the recommended cap to 32 may cost nothing visible in practice, but its measured benefit is also an upper bound.
- The 6x-throttle saturation finding (61 of 714 notifications/s serviced) is a measurement of a synthetic MessageChannel pump, not of a live 20-seat devnet feed through a real WebSocket and the real decoder. The decode itself (6.87 us/payload, measured elsewhere) is not in my harness, so the real machine is if anything slightly worse. Nobody has yet profiled a browser against a live 20-seat feed; that remains the honest gap.
- The lobby, gate, muster and settle phases were not measured — only the fight, which is the dense one. The camera pan from LOBBY to FIGHTING (spec §1.4) animates a transform on `#camera` while the whole scene is mounted, and it was not exercised.
- `Arena.tsx:463`'s bullet render is where the cap goes, and it is the same expression whose `ref` callback populates the frame loop's `nodes` Map. A cap that changes which slot indices are drawn without keeping the Map keyed on the true slot index will desync the extrapolator from the chain's bullet array. Cap by taking the first N ACTIVE slots and keep `slot` as the key.
