# Art-direction critique: shipped HEARTROT arena vs IMAGE A (crisp pixel knights) and IMAGE B (33 Immortals composition)

## Findings

The rig is built and correct; the PICTURE is not. Nine fixes, ranked. Seven are cosmetic and small. Do them in this order.

**1. Grade the boss, or the composition never reads as IMAGE B.** `Boss.tsx` applies no filter to the creature. The art is warm pale pink/mauve — `#c9aab0` (sRGB L 177), `#af8c92` (148), `#936975` (115) — and the graded temple tops out at L 68.7. The boss is the brightest, warmest, most saturated object in a cavern graded to ~210° blue. In IMAGE B the demon is DARK and rim-lit; here it is a pastel sticker pasted on a cold cave. Fix: `style={{ filter: BOSS_GRADE }}` on the `#art` `<g>` inside `.hr-boss-shell` — a cold desaturating grade to bring the body under the pit's ceiling — then paint the rim light back deliberately (a `<use>` of the silhouette offset −2 units, `#9fe8ff` at 0.25) and leave core + eyes as the only warm/bright things. One node, one filter, zero chain.

**2. Stop the creature painting the pit floor.** Measured: **33.8% of walkable pit-floor area is covered by boss art at mean sRGB L 133 (p25/p50/p75 = 97/148/177)** against a graded floor of ~L 53. `legs` (world x 437..788, y 436..608, 6,028 px, mean L 132) fills the centre of the arena; `mace` (170..503) and `claws` (677..833) take the flanks. Contiguously x 170..833 = 67% of pit width. IMAGE B's pit is a dark hole with only the hands entering at the rim. Fix, no chain change: `HIDDEN.add('legs')` (it is `index: null`, nothing raycasts it) and raise the boss clip from `PIT_BOT + 1` (608) to `PIT_TOP + 48` (432), so only the hands cross the lip. `mace`/`claws` are indexed and must stay drawn — clip them at the rim, not the gate.

**3. The knights are below the legibility floor.** Measured relative-luminance contrast against the pit floor at its p50: **Nocturne 1.91:1, Argent 2.11:1, Cobalt 2.54:1.** `Scene.tsx`'s header claims "ally 4.35:1" — that number was measured against `PAL`'s primitive-circle colours, not the shipped sprite art. Compounding it, `mode_downsample(…, 2)` pushes the dark keyline from 36.2% → **49.0%** of Argent's pixels and 40.1% → 48.7% of Nocturne's, while the identifying accent survives at 40–56 pixels per knight (Cobalt blue `#2f5585` 56 px / 7.5%; Argent silver `#b4b4c1` 40 px / 6.1%). Three skins converge to three dark blobs. Fix without touching `PLAYER_HIT_RADIUS`: brighten the pit pool (raise `scene-pool` stop 0 from 0.2 to ~0.35) and add a per-skin key light — one extra `<use href="#kN-sil">` behind the body, 1 unit up-left, filled with the skin's accent at 0.5. That restores skin identity at 21 px without re-tracing.

**4. Give the sealed core a resting state.** `Boss.tsx`'s `.hr-boss-vent` is `opacity: 0` while sealed, so IMAGE B's bright cyan chest orb — the focal point of the entire reference — **does not exist during the lobby, the muster, the spawn reveal, or the first ~65% of the fight** (the vent opens only after 11,700 of 18,000 shell damage). Meanwhile `Scene.tsx` paints a permanent 330-unit `#6ee1ff` radial spill centred on exactly `(587, 346)`: a glow with no lamp. `styles.css:373-386` already contains the correct two-state design (`.core-glow` sealed = `--cyan-dim` at 0.35, `.is-open` = `--cyan` + pulse) — it is dead CSS. Port those two rules into `Boss.tsx`'s `CSS` block, keyed on `.hr-vent-open`.

**5. Wire up the dead stylesheet — it is where the depth cues went.** `.boss-part`, `.core-glow`, `.scene-bg`, `.pit-rim`, `.boss-eye`, `#boss-breathe` appear ONLY in `styles.css`; nothing in `app/src/render/` renders any of those names (the live rig uses `hr-`-prefixed classes, none of which appear in `styles.css`). Three consequences: (a) `.pit-rim { filter: drop-shadow(0 -2px 0 rgb(159 199 232 / 0.22)) }` — the lit near edge of the foreground plane, the strongest depth cue in IMAGE B — never applies; `Arena.tsx`'s `rim` memo is two flat greys, `#2b2733` and `#413a4f`, with no lit edge. **One word fixes it: `className="pit-rim"` on that `<g>`.** (b) `.boss-part { will-change: transform }` never applies, so the 13 boss part groups covering ~95% of frame area and taking every WAAPI flinch/break one-shot are **unpromoted** — spec §9.2 rule 4 calls this "the whole game" (30–85× on software raster) and open risk #1 says measure it first. Add `willChange: 'transform'` to the part `<g>` style in `Boss.tsx:224`. (c) two conflicting temple grades exist: `--temple-grade: brightness(0.62) contrast(1.06) sepia(0.38) hue-rotate(168deg) saturate(1.45)` (dead) vs `TEMPLE_GRADE = sepia(1) hue-rotate(177deg) saturate(1.39) brightness(0.5)` (live). Delete one.

**6. Nothing on screen is actually crisp.** `usePixelFit` sets `crispEdges` only when `min(box) · dpr ≥ 1024`. On any normal laptop the stage is under 1024 css px tall, so `crisp` is **false in practice** and the whole scene renders `geometricPrecision` — antialiased. Underneath that, the temple is stretched `1024/210 = 4.876` × `1024/238 = 4.303`: non-integer AND anisotropic, so no source pixel can ever land on a device-pixel boundary at any zoom. The knight pipeline is 62×82 source px → 31×41 after halving → ~21×28 css px at a 700 px stage: **~88% of source pixels discarded**, then antialiased. IMAGE A's "crisp blocky pixels, heavy dark outlines" survive none of that. Fix: re-emit the temple on a 1024-divisible canvas (256×256 → integer ×4) so `crispEdges` has something to snap to, and treat sub-1:1 as the common case rather than the degraded one.

**7. Flat lighting on every knight.** `Knight.tsx:376` draws an identical `<ellipse cy={20} rx={11} ry={4}>` in `PAL.outline` at 0.45 on all twenty seats, with no offset, no length, and no relation to the scene's only light source — the core at world `(587, 346)`. Twenty identical stamps read as stickers, not bodies in a lit pit. Fix, no new node: offset the ellipse by the normalised vector from `CORE_WORLD` to the seat and scale `rx` by distance — the light position is already imported in `Scene.tsx`.

**8. A hard amputation, dead centre of frame.** The boss is clipped at `y = PIT_BOT + 1 = 608`, and the rim occluder is `MAP_WALL_PATH` clipped to rows 36–37 — which are open at `x = 30..33` (the doorway, world x 480..543). Measured: **70% of that 64×33-unit window is boss art**, so the creature's legs get a straight horizontal cut with nothing in front of them, in the middle of the screen, at the exact tile the player walks through. Fixed for free by (2); otherwise the occluder needs a solid lintel over the doorway.

**9. The pit is a rectangle and the fight camera wastes 39% of the frame.** `arena.json` rows 24–32 span x 1..62 dead flat; only rows 33/34/35 are shaved 2/4/7 tiles, and only at the bottom. `Scene.tsx` then draws a **992-unit perfectly straight** `#a8d6f0` line at `PIT_TOP`. Nothing reads as IMAGE B's ellipse. And `CAM_ARENA = scale(1) translate(0,0)` shows world y 608..1008 — 400 units, 39% of the fight frame — of empty lobby approach that nobody stands in. Cheapest fix is camera-only and cosmetic: `CAM_ARENA = scale(1.249) translate(-102px, 0)` frames world x 102..922, y 0..820, keeps the whole boss (painted bbox x 170..851) and the whole pit depth, and halves the dead band. Re-shaping the pit ellipse is the better fix but it is an on-chain change (see below).

Also worth one line each: the walk is a **shear, not a gait** — `LEG_BAND_TOP = 0.62` slides the bottom 16 of 42 rows two columns sideways, and that band is **50% of Cobalt's painted pixels including the shield and cape**, so the lower half of the body slides laterally instead of legs stepping; and the boss eye is **stored three times with three colours and two coordinates** — `Boss.tsx` (138,82)/(145,82) cyan→red, `Spawn.tsx` (137,81)/(144,81) `#8fe9ff`, `styles.css .boss-eye` `--ember #ff5a4a` — so the spawn flare lands 3 arena units off the resting eye it is supposed to ignite.

## On-chain

Eight of the nine fixes are purely cosmetic and touch no account, no instruction and no generated table:

- boss grade filter, rim light `<use>`, core resting state, `.pit-rim` classname, `will-change` on the part groups, knight key light and directional shadow, camera framing, eye-coordinate de-duplication, and the temple canvas re-emit. All of these are presentation only. A client rendering none of them still plays the same game on the same tick — which is the line the spec draws.
- `HIDDEN.add('legs')` and raising the boss clip are safe specifically because `legs`, `ground`, `torso` and `core` carry `index: null` in `assets/sprites/hitboxes.json` and are never raycast. Hiding them moves no hitbox and changes no damage. The clip must NOT be tightened past a part that IS indexed — `mace` (index 7, world y 301..712) and `claws` (index 8, 286..616) are raycast targets and hiding drawn art the chain still resolves hits against re-creates the exact "drawn boss ≠ raycast boss" defect `gen_hitboxes.py` exists to prevent.
- The pit-floor pool brightness, the scene gradients and the temple grade are client-local. Twenty players may legitimately have different accessibility settings; none of it decides damage.

Two items ARE on chain and must not be done casually:

- **Re-shaping the pit into an actual ellipse** means editing `assets/map/arena.json` and re-running `tools/gen_map.py`, which regenerates `programs/heartrot/src/map.rs` and its TS mirror. `PIT_TOP`/`PIT_BOT` gate `move_player`, the wall bitboard gates `raycast`, and the spawn/entrance reachability asserts must be re-validated. Shaving the pit's top rows is especially dangerous: rows 1..23 must stay open floor or `raycast` dies on a wall before it reaches a part. Not worth it for a silhouette — do the camera fix (9) instead, which buys most of the read for zero chain risk.
- **Any change to `BOSS_SCALE`** is a `gen_hitboxes.py --scale` argument that rewrites `PART_HITBOXES`, `CORE_X/Y/RADIUS_SQ`, `MUZZLES` and `BOSS_ANCHOR_*` in both `hitboxes.rs` and `hitboxes.ts`. Making the boss wider than 66.5% to match IMAGE B would move every muzzle and every hitbox, and Rust and TS must ship in the same commit or predicted and actual volleys diverge. The spec pinned S=3 and named S=2 as the only sanctioned lever. I am not recommending a scale change: fixes 1, 2 and 5 buy the "looming" read at S=3 by lighting and occlusion, which is how the reference gets it too.

Nothing in this critique asks for a new byte on any account. No animation clock, no `is_moving`, no camera field, no damage log.

## Risks

- Fix 2 (hiding `legs`, raising the boss clip to the rim) changes the creature's read from full-body to bust-plus-hands. That IS the IMAGE B composition, but it discards ~6,000 drawn pixels of art the user supplied. Show it to them before committing — this is the one fix that removes art rather than lighting it.
- Fix 1 (grading the boss) is a global CSS filter and will hit the same wall `Scene.tsx` already documents for the temple: a single filter cannot separate the creature's warm flesh tones from its cool armour. If the graded boss goes muddy, the escape hatch is the same one Scene names — a per-fill remap over the parsed `PART_SRC` strings at module scope — not a PNG and not a new generator.
- Fix 3 raises the pit pool brightness, which will lift the boss art sitting on that floor too and partially undo fix 1. Do 1 and 2 first, re-measure the knight contrast against the resulting floor, and only then decide how much pool lift is still needed. Measuring these two independently will give the wrong answer.
- Fix 5(b) adds `will-change: transform` to 13 groups covering ~95% of frame area. Spec open risk #1 says the S=3 raster area is UNMEASURED, and 13 promoted layers at that size is a real memory cost on a dPR-2 display, which nobody in this project has tested. It is almost certainly right per spec §9.2 rule 4, but it is the one fix here that could make frame time worse rather than better, and it deserves the measurement the spec already asked for.
- The `Scene.tsx` header's WCAG figures (self 8.02:1, ally 4.35:1, bullet 6.37:1) are wrong for the shipped renderer — they describe the deleted primitive circles. Anyone re-deriving lighting from that comment will reach the same wrong conclusion I nearly did. Correct or delete the comment in whatever commit touches the pit lighting.
- I opened no browser and timed no frame. Every finding is geometry, colour arithmetic or grep over the shipped source. The composition and contrast claims are solid; any claim about how a fix will FEEL in motion is not measured, and the shear-vs-gait and antialiasing items in particular want one screenshot at 8x before anyone spends a day on them.
